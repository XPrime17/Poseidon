When an engagement is ending (pilot wrap-up, client offboarding, contract end), the *exit week* is the highest-leverage moment to ask for a testimonial — goodwill is at its peak and decays fast after the relationship formally closes.

**Why:** Live engagements have ongoing asks; clients are stingy with the "yes" budget. At exit, the relationship is closing and they're emotionally ready to give one parting favor. Miss this window and the response rate plummets within ~2 weeks of offboarding.

**How to apply (the capture flow):**

1. **Channel ranking when video is off the table** (which it usually is for executive/director-level clients):
   - **Primary: LinkedIn recommendation** — identity-bound, public, third-party-feeling, async, ~2 min effort, lives on the recommender's profile (stronger trust signal than a quote you control).
   - **Fallback: written quote** with permission for a website testimonials section (attribute with name + title + logo).
   - **Skip Google review** unless the business has a Google Business Profile *and* the buyer demographic googles you (B2B franchise/multi-unit operators usually don't).
   - **Skip video** unless the client is unusually warm. Don't bundle the ask with a Zoom — that's a friction multiplier.

2. **One email per client.** Three sentences max:
   - Thank them
   - Ask for a LinkedIn recommendation, *with a pre-drafted version they can edit*
   - Offer the email-reply fallback: "if LinkedIn isn't your thing, just reply 'use that as a quote on the site' and I'll attribute it."

3. **The pre-draft is the unlock.** Blank-page paralysis — not willingness — is the actual friction. Most people will happily edit your draft but won't write one from scratch. Ship the draft inside the same email as the ask.

4. **Reference example** (client → service provider shape, scroll to "From a Client to Business/Vendor"): https://trustmary.com/testimonials/linkedin-testimonial-examples/

**Pattern shape for the draft** (~70 words, name a problem solved + a specific result + a "who should hire them" line):

> "[Vendor] went above and beyond during our [engagement]. We brought them in to [solve specific problem], and within [timeframe] [specific outcome]. [Vendor contact] was responsive, took feedback seriously, and treated our [business] like their own. If you're a [target buyer description] and you're [specific pain], talk to them."

**Pitfalls:**
- Don't bundle multiple asks (LinkedIn rec + Google review + case study interview = 0 responses).
- Don't ask before the engagement is clearly ending — premature asks read as needy.
- Don't write a 5-paragraph rec template; clients will balk at the length and post nothing.
