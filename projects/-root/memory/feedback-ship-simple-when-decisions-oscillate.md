When a decision keeps oscillating across sessions (e.g., TourForce pricing flipped between 2 and 3 tiers four times, v1–v7), treat the oscillation itself as the signal: the call can't be resolved from theory because the grounding data doesn't exist yet. Stop re-analyzing. Ship the simplest defensible version, get real signal, let the market arbitrate.

**Why:** Scott circled the 2-vs-3-tier question repeatedly with no live customer data (Leaside inbound not active, NV outbound weak). On 2026-05-22 he accepted "lock it in for now" on the 2-tier model — explicitly provisional. The value wasn't picking the perfect structure; it was recognizing that more analysis wouldn't help and committing to a reversible choice that generates data.

**How to apply:** When you notice Scott reversing a prior decision (especially the 3rd+ time), name the pattern explicitly rather than just helping him flip again. Reframe from "which option is right?" to "what's the cheapest way to get the data that would settle this?" Recommend shipping the simplest version with a defined trigger for revisiting (e.g., "add a 3rd tier if prospects keep asking for a middle"). Pair the recommendation with an honest cost of the simplification — don't only sell the upside. Relates to [[tourforce-pricing-model]].
